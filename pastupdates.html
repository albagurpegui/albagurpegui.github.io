02.03.2026: Publication in IEEE TAC

I am super happy to announce that our paper 'Minimax Linear Regulator Problems for Positive Systems’ has been recently accepted for publication as full paper in IEEE Transactions on Automatic control. This work presents a deterministic, multi-disturbance minimax Linear Regulator (LR) framework for positive linear time-invariant systems in continuous time.

Similar to the Linear-Quadratic Regulator (LQR), this framework can be applied to the stabilization of positive systems. We show that this problem class admits explicit solutions in both finite and infinite horizon settings, resulting in vector-valued ODEs and algebraic equations, respectively. These characterize optimal control policies that take the form of a time-varying state feedback law in the finite horizon case and a static feedback law in the infinite horizon case. Unlike LQR, the number of unknowns in the explicit solution grows only linearly with the state dimension. Furthermore, the sparsity structure of the optimal control policy is inherited from a matrix in the problem constraints, allowing the problem designer to determine the sparsity of the resulting optimal control laws a priori.

The full manuscript can be found published in IEEE TAC and in arXiv.

12.01.2026-17.04.2026: Visiting Scholar at Purdue University

I visited Professor Takashi Tanaka's lab at the School of Aeronautics and Astronautics. During this research visit I have been involved in two main projects that extend my work on L1 optimal control for positive systems to the stochastic and general-sum settings. In particular, this works demonstrate that the Linear Regulator framework is robust to multiplicative stochastic uncertainty and characterize the feedback and stationary Nash equilibria for a general-sum formulation. During my time here I am also joining the group meetings of professor Philip Paré who encouraged me to participate in the ICON Student Research Conference.
